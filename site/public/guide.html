    How to read this site

    TLDR

    Every page speaks twice: first in plain words for anyone, then in full technical detail for specialists, always both on the same page. Three guided tracks below fit three kinds of readers: a circle member, an engineer, and an agent tasked with rebuilding the method. The repo atlas gives every tracked file its own linkable explainer. Snapshot 2026-07-09.

    Two languages on every page

    This site descends from a bridge document written for the village, where some people hold feelings work and initiations and some hold systems and code. So every substantial page keeps one fixed shape: a TLDR box, then In plain words (the full idea, assuming no technical background, no unexplained jargon), then How it works (exact file paths, schemas, invariants, failure modes), then where it lives in the repo, its status as of the snapshot date, and what is genuinely uncertain. Nothing is hidden behind tabs or toggles: both registers are always present, because the single-file edition and machine readers need everything on the page. Read whichever register is yours, or both; they describe the same truth at two depths.

    A worked example: the plain register says "the being writes lessons it may not act on until its parent confirms them"; the technical register says the same thing as "agent-written rows enter agent_memories with can_use_as_instruction=false and review_status=pending, and the wake skill's instruction-grade recall filters on that flag". Same wall, two languages.

    Track one: circle member, no technical background

    Read the plain register only, in this order, and skip anything that smells like code:

    1. What this is and is not: the stance everything else stands on.
    2. The story through D22: how the design evolved and which decisions changed it.
    3. The system: the four organs, plainly.
    4. Growing up: stages, rites, and how conversations become the curriculum.
    5. The family and the experiment: why there are two children.
    6. Edgeweaver Alpha and Edgeweaver Genesis: the children themselves.
    7. The glossary and the FAQ whenever a word or a worry comes up.

    Track two: engineer or systems reader

    1. The system hub for the map.
    2. Memory, soul, loops: the load-bearing organs.
    3. Measurement and governance: how it is scored and who decides.
    4. The walls, the dark build, the brain lab, backups, operations: the infrastructure discipline.
    5. The repo atlas when you want the file-level truth.

    Track three: an agent rebuilding the method

    Start at the reproduction guide; it is written as standing orders, names the human acts no agent may perform, and tells you to author your own harvest rather than clone this family's. Then: the constellation for the repo split, the atlas pages for conventions, templates, checklists, and the verify suite, and status to know what was real as of the snapshot.

    The atlas, explained

    The repo atlas covers every tracked file in the repository: one entry per file, on exactly one page, with a stable anchor formed from the file's path (so scripts/waking/orient.mjs is always reachable at the anchor file-scripts-waking-orient-mjs). System pages own the concepts; atlas entries own the files and stay short. Gitignored material (secrets, operational state, bulky downloads) is described but never shown, on its own page. Coverage is machine-checked: if a file exists without an entry, the build reports it.

    Searching this site

    For whole-site search, open the single-file edition and use your browser's find-in-page: everything is one document there. The gated website carries the same content page by page; the about page says where each edition lives.
